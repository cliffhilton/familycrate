Section 03
Chores & Tasks

Chores in FamilyCrate are tied to specific people, time slots, and point values. Kids check them off themselves — no nagging required. Points are awarded automatically on completion.

1
Adding a chore
Tap + Add and select "Chore/Task." Give it a name, assign it to one or more family members, set a point value, time slot, and repeat schedule. Chores appear on the calendar at their assigned time in each person's column.
Point tip: Keep point values between 3–15 per chore and set your dollar rate lower (like $0.10/pt). A full week of chores might earn 150 pts = $15. Feels big to kids, manageable for you.
2
How kids check off chores
Kids tap their chore block on the calendar and tap the checkbox to mark it done. A checkmark appears and points are immediately added to their total. They can see their running point total in the member chip at the top of the screen.
👦 Kids love this part. Seeing their points go up instantly is the whole motivation loop. Make sure the leaderboard is visible so they can track each other.
3
Repeat schedules
Set chores to repeat Daily (every day), Weekly (same day each week), or Monthly (same date each month). The chore reappears automatically — no re-adding needed. At the end of a reward period, chores reset to unchecked.
4
Categories
Organize chores and tasks under custom categories (Chores, School, Personal, etc.). Manage categories in Settings. Each category gets its own tab in the Lists view, keeping things tidy as your list grows.
⚠️
Important: Chores are different from calendar events. Chores earn points and reset with the reward period. Events are for scheduling only — they don't earn points or reset.

Section 04
Points & Dollar Values

FamilyCrate separates points from dollars intentionally. Kids see exciting high point numbers. Only parents see the dollar conversion — giving you full control over the real value without deflating the excitement.

The Points Philosophy

High point values (5–15 per chore) feel motivating to kids. A lower dollar rate ($0.05–$0.15/pt) keeps payouts reasonable. A full week of effort earning 150 pts = $15 feels like a real achievement — and it is.

⭐
Earned Points
Points earned by completing chores during the current reward period. Shown on the leaderboard and in each member's chip at the top of the screen.
🛍️
Spent Points
Points used to redeem rewards. Shown below each member's earned total on the leaderboard. Resets when you start a new reward period.
💵
Dollar Value
Earned points × your dollar rate. Only visible to parents. Kids see points — you see dollars. Set your rate in Settings → Points & Dollars.
$
Setting your point rate
Go to Settings → Points & Dollars. Enter a dollar value per point (e.g. $0.10). Tap Save. This applies to all family members. You can adjust it anytime — changes take effect immediately on the leaderboard.
🔄
The Reward Period
The reward period is a defined window (default 14 days) during which points accumulate. At the end, you hit Reset Period in Settings — this clears completed chore checkmarks, zeroes spent points, and starts a fresh period. Think of it like a pay cycle.
Tip: Reset on the same day each period (every 2 weeks on Sunday, for example) so kids know when to expect payouts.

Section 05
Rewards Store

The Rewards Store is where kids spend their points. They browse available rewards, submit a request, and you approve. No surprise spending — every redemption goes through you.

1
Setting up rewards
In Settings (PIN required), find the Rewards Store section. Tap + Add reward. Give it a title, point cost, and icon. Good starter rewards: Screen time, Pick dinner, Stay up late, Movie pick, Cash out points, Skip a chore.
Tip: Set point costs high enough that rewards feel earned. If chores average 8 pts each, a reward costing 50 pts = about 6 chores worth of work. That feels meaningful.
2
How kids request rewards
Kids tap the Points tab (⭐) and then Rewards Store. They browse available rewards, tap one, select themselves, and submit a request. They can only request rewards they have enough points for — the button is disabled if they can't afford it.
👦 Kids see their available balance next to each member button when requesting. No guessing — they know exactly what they can afford.
3
Approving rewards (parent)
Go to Settings (PIN required). Pending requests appear in the Reward Requests section on the right column. Each request shows the reward name, who requested it, point cost, and the date/time it was submitted.
4
Approve & Schedule
When you tap Approve, a scheduling modal pops up with the reward title pre-filled. Pick a date and time to add it to the calendar as a 🎉 gold event — so the whole family sees it coming. Or tap Approve Only to approve without scheduling (good for rewards that need coordination, like "Have a friend over").
🎉 When a reward lands on the calendar, it shows as a glowing gold block with the kid's name. When they tap it, they see a festive "Earned Reward!" popup. It feels like a celebration.
📋
Reward Bank — Approved rewards appear in the Reward Bank section in Settings. This shows all rewards approved during the current period. When you reset the period, the bank clears automatically. You can also schedule or archive individual rewards from the bank anytime.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why FamilyCrate?

How is FamilyCrate different from Cozi, FamilyWall, or other family apps? ▾
Most family calendar apps stop at scheduling. Cozi and FamilyWall are solid for shared calendars but don't have chore tracking, points, or a rewards system. Apps like S'moresUp and Chores & Allowance Bot focus on chores and allowance but lack a real family calendar. FamilyCrate is the only app that combines all of it — calendar, chores, points, rewards store, and leaderboard — in one place. No switching between apps.
Why do points work better than cash allowance? ▾
Points create a visible, immediate feedback loop. Kids see their total go up the moment they check a chore — that instant reward is powerful. Cash feels abstract until it's in their hand. Points also let you set high, exciting numbers (150 pts!) while keeping the actual dollar value manageable. And the leaderboard makes it competitive in the best way.
We've tried chore charts before and they always fizzle out. Why will this be different? ▾
Three things make FamilyCrate stick where charts don't: (1) It lives on devices everyone already uses every day. (2) The rewards store gives kids something real to work toward — not just a gold star. (3) The leaderboard creates natural sibling competition. The system is designed around what actually motivates kids, not what looks good on a refrigerator.
Is FamilyCrate good for homeschool families? ▾
It was actually built by a homeschool dad for exactly this use case. The calendar handles co-op schedules, class times, and activities. Chores can include school tasks like reading assignments. The flexible categories let you organize everything from academics to household duties in one place.

Using the App

How do I get started in 5 minutes? ▾
1. Register at familycrate.co/register — takes 2 minutes. 2. Add your family members with their colors. 3. Set your PIN in Settings. 4. Add 3–5 recurring chores with point values. 5. Create 3–4 rewards in the Rewards Store. That's it — you're live. Add more over time as you find what works.
Do kids need their own login? ▾
No. Kids access FamilyCrate through the same family app — they just select their name when checking off chores or requesting rewards. There's no separate kid account to manage. On a shared device like a family iPad, everyone uses the same app.
How do reward periods work? ▾
A reward period is the window during which points accumulate — default is 14 days. At the end, you reset the period (Settings → Reward Period → Reset). This clears chore checkmarks so chores show as undone again, zeroes spent points, and starts the clock fresh. Think of it like a two-week pay cycle. Before resetting, pay out any cash owed and approve pending rewards.
Can I use FamilyCrate on multiple devices at the same time? ▾
Yes — that's one of FamilyCrate's strengths. Open it on the family iPad, your phone, and your MacBook simultaneously. Changes sync in real time. Check a chore on the iPad and it updates on Mom's phone instantly. No refresh needed.
What's the difference between a chore and an event? ▾
Chores earn points and reset with the period. They're assigned to specific people and appear as checkable blocks on the calendar. Events are for scheduling — they appear on the calendar for everyone to see but don't earn points and don't reset. Use chores for anything you want kids to earn points for. Use events for appointments, activities, and family schedule items.
Can kids request a reward they can't afford? ▾
No. The request button is disabled if a kid doesn't have enough points. Their available balance (earned minus already-spent, including pending requests) is shown next to their name when they browse the rewards store. No sneaking in requests they haven't earned.
